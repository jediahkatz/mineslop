# Renewable kelp slice

This implements a playable kelp loop, not the complete world/item or ocean
expansion. It uses native kelp already generated by the unchanged shared marine
generator in versions 4–7. No generator, golden, registry ID, cell state mask or
old terrain byte changes.

## Player loop

Break and collect a native tip, retaining the lower plant, or harvest the column
and spend a collected kelp through `GameUseActions.place` to replant it. A
supported tip extends after **487.5 active dimension seconds**. Break and collect
that extension and repeat: the base remains, while the inventory earns surplus.
The existing explicit break restores source water. Existing support-loss
removal still commits the World and actual `DropOverflow` retention atomically;
pickup collection still transfers ownership into `Gameplay`, never a shadow
inventory.

The existing resource chain is exercised with that collected surplus:

- One kelp + 10 furnace seconds -> one dried kelp; real fuel is consumed.
- Dried kelp takes its registered **0.8-second** held-use cycle, restores one
  hunger and 0.6 saturation, and consumes one item. Ordinary foods retain their
  1.6-second default. No new registry item is introduced.
- Nine dried kelp -> one dried-kelp block; unpacking returns exactly nine.
- A dried-kelp block burns for 200 furnace seconds. It is consumed once, with
  input, output, remaining burn time and stored furnace XP owned by Settlement.

## Timing and ecology policy

Java's 14% random-tick chance at default randomTickSpeed 3 gives approximately
487.6 seconds per extension. This implementation is an honest **fixed-cadence
bounded equivalent**, rounded to 1,950 existing 0.25-second fluid ticks. It is
not Java's geometric waiting-time distribution or a one-second farm.

- A newly observed native/replanted/exposed tip gets a deterministic coordinate,
  dimension and seed-derived initial age 0–24 and a full cooldown.
- A successful extension transfers that one saved timer to the new tip, adds one
  to its age, and starts another full cooldown. Age 25 cannot extend.
- Cutting the top exposes a newly initialized tip, permitting renewal. An actual
  published break retires the old timer, including break/replant between ticks.
- Ordinary neighbor/recovery/admission wakes can check support early but cannot
  shorten, restart or spend a saved growth deadline.
- Blocked/maximum-age/maximum-height attempts start a new full check interval,
  without a World edit. Clearing an obstruction does not grant immediate growth.
- A 26-cell maximum natural height also bounds downward root reads, including
  already-tall manually placed columns. Existing taller cells are preserved.
- Growth checks actual root support, not just the presence of a kelp immediately
  below it. An uprooted chain waiting for retained-drop capacity cannot grow.
- Natural extension uses the same source/falling-water rule as manual placement.
  Lateral levels, air, and waterlogged structural hosts do not qualify.
  **Bubble columns are source-like and explicitly convert to ordinary source
  kelp**, as in existing paid placement. Magma is not valid root support.
- Unloaded cells are unknown, never air. Their exact timer survives, with bounded
  later checks and the existing admission/recovery path. There is no generation
  on read or accumulated multi-growth catch-up. The clock is active dimension
  time, not a per-plant resident-time clock: an overdue unloaded tip can make one
  attempt after re-admission, then must wait another full interval.
- Pausing, death, non-simulating frames and inactive dimensions do not advance
  their scheduler or fractional tick. The real Game frame already gates hidden
  frames and clamps suspended wall time.

## Budgets, fairness and publication

Production defaults remain per dimension:

| Work | Unchanged cap |
| --- | ---: |
| Total fluid + marine proposals per tick | 96 |
| Distinct prerequisite reads per proposal | 192 |
| Admission/recovery scan cells per update | 256 |
| Ticks per update | 4 at 0.25 seconds |
| Total queued cells, including kelp timers | 4,096 |
| Dirty sections / scan jobs / recovery regions | 256 / 32 / 16 |
| Scan-job / resident-recovery visits per update | 64 / 16 |

Long-lived kelp timers use at most one quarter of queue capacity, capped at
1,024. All other slots remain available to ordinary water work. Timed cells are
pinned during fallible preparation and rotate to the back after selection;
repeated wakes or vetoes cannot permanently hold the head of the queue. Recovery
scans now run after ready work is consumed, so an external wake flood cannot
refill a small pool before every scan and freeze its cursor.

Growth's target water may also have a water proposal in the same tick. The
aquatic replacement wins that coordinate; the World never receives duplicate
changes that would endlessly veto both. World, required drop owners, and the
prepared scheduler participant commit together. The new tip record is installed
before notifications, including synchronous autosave and throwing observers.
World/budget/incarnation/support vetoes retain the mature opportunity for retry;
they never spend the next age or create inventory rewards.

The existing fixed scheduler byte reservation is unchanged. A timer's extra
projection plus its queue tuple fits the existing 128-byte per-cell envelope.
Tests cover extreme signed coordinates and maximum clocks across all dimensions.
No whole-ocean scan or new recurring full-world traversal is introduced.

Bounded admission is deliberate: when the marine quota is full, additional tips
retain conservative section/region recovery work until an exact timer slot is
freed by a confirmed removal or change into a body cell. Dormant, terminal and
unloaded timers are not evicted just to fabricate new ages. This does not promise
simultaneous renewal of every plant in an arbitrarily large explored ocean.

## Save projection and migration

The outer fluid sidecar stays version 1. Every original queue remains an ordered
seven-tuple array:

```text
[x, y, z, wakeDue, expand, coralId, coralDue]
```

An active dimension optionally adds:

```json
{
  "marine": {
    "version": 1,
    "kelp": [[8, 1, 8, 12, 1951]]
  }
}
```

Each kelp tuple is `[x, y, z, tipAge, growthDue]` and must refer to an existing
queue cell, not extra hidden queued work. The ordinary wake deadline may be
earlier than growthDue; it cannot bypass it. Only absent new state initializes
empty. Explicit null/undefined, unknown projection keys/version, malformed
tuples, duplicate or unqueued cells, simultaneous coral/kelp claims, invalid ages,
clocks and out-of-dimension coordinates reject before live owner replacement.

Normal/default legacy loads retain every old seven-tuple, ordering, flag and
coral deadline verbatim. Old kelp cells receive a full cooldown only when actually
evaluated; no historical growth or age is invented. New snapshots round-trip
ages, deadlines, ordinary queues and fractional time. World-file export/parse,
archive preflight, fresh GameFluidServices construction and an isolated IndexedDB
cold load are covered.

The existing opt-in smaller-pool behavior may conservatively coarsen *ordinary
water* work and restart scan cursors. Exact marine/coral timer records are
prioritized and never silently coarsened: an undersized timer pool rejects
atomically. In particular, a one-slot custom queue cannot admit a persistent
kelp timer and still reserve water capacity. Production uses the unchanged
4,096-slot pool.

Parent GUI procedure, in a fresh main-app browser profile/disposable world:

1. Import a generated v7 fixture, retaining the archive's actual version.
   Do not replace the user's original browser/world.
2. Observe the retained stem at y48, source water at y49, two collected kelp,
   and the partial saved cooldown. Surface for air while waiting: this is a
   survival save, so staying submerged until death would freeze growth.
   Stay nearby and active; pause briefly and confirm that pause does not
   complete it.
3. Wait the remaining active time. One kelp extends into y49. Harvest it through
   actual input and swim to collect: the inventory increases from two to three,
   while the lower plant remains. No GUI/performance success is implied by the
   CPU fixtures.
4. Spend one finite kelp to replant a cleared supported source/falling cell.
   Confirm lateral flow and magma fail without losing the item.
5. Use an actually acquired furnace/fuel and crafting table to dry, eat, compact,
   unpack and burn surplus; export/reload during partial growth or cooking.
   The CPU resource-chain test explicitly declares its starting stations and two
   coal; it does not claim native station acquisition.

## Still open

The broader world/item, mob, progression, building and performance expansion
remains open. This does not add bone meal (no registered `BONE_MEAL`), reef/coral
renewal, seagrass support/shears parity, pickup buoyancy or new ocean recipes.
Solid coral remains finite native loot: no live-fragment-to-coral-block recipe
is invented. Java's full water shortest-drop search and exact stochastic random
ticks remain separate. Native World admission repair belongs to the parent, as
do main-app GUI and performance acceptance. Existing unrelated ecology-native
and rendering failures must not be weakened or relabeled as kelp acceptance.
